Abstract

In modern sericulture, the domesticated silkworm (Bombyx mori) is an obligate monophagous herbivore whose larval survival, metabolic vigor, and economic silk gland synthesis depend entirely on the biochemical integrity of fresh white mulberry (Morus alba L.) foliage. Fungal foliar epidemics—predominantly Powdery Mildew (Phyllactinia corylea), Leaf Rust (Cerotelium fici), and Cercospora Leaf Spot (Cercospora moricola)—inflict severe biological degradation on host leaves, reducing physiological leaf moisture from optimal levels of 70–75% down to under 50%, while depleting crude protein concentrations from >22% to under 14%. When fed to 3rd, 4th, or 5th instar silkworms, such diseased leaves precipitate catastrophic digestive flacherie, septicemia, and up to 80% rearing tray mortality. Concurrently, inadvertent harvesting of leaves shortly after fungicide spraying without observing mandatory 10–14 day chemical withholding periods causes lethal neurotoxic collapse across commercial rearing houses.

To eliminate this systemic sericultural bottleneck without requiring expensive laboratory spectroscopy or unreliable field travel, we developed SilkGuard, an edge-native, zero-framework bio-computational platform. SilkGuard couples deep learning computer vision with sericultural pathology, deploying an optimized MobileNetV2 convolutional neural network (3.4M parameters, 300 MFLOPs) that performs real-time foliar diagnostics directly inside client web browsers with sub-40ms latency. The architecture incorporates an automated 70% Confidence Safety Gate to prevent false negative misclassifications, alongside Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to visualize convolutional feature saliency across fungal pustules and chlorotic halos. Additionally, SilkGuard features an interactive 24-specimen calibrated benchmark dataset, real-time data augmentation pipeline, dynamic 20-epoch training convergence engine, a live silkworm feeding risk simulator, and bilingual English–Kannada agronomic advisory generation. SilkGuard establishes an end-to-end bridge between molecular plant pathology and deployed edge software engineering.

1. Introduction and Project Overview

1.1 Introduction

Sericulture represents an agro-based economic lifeline across Karnataka, contributing significantly to rural employment in high-density sericultural districts such as Ramanagara, Kanakapura, Channapatna, Kolar, and Mandya. The domestic silk industry relies fundamentally on the health and nutritional yield of mulberry (Morus alba L.), a fast-growing deciduous woody perennial whose green foliage constitutes the exclusive, non-negotiable food source for the silkworm Bombyx mori. Because Bombyx mori has undergone millennia of captive artificial selection, it has lost its wild survival instincts and completely depends on sericulturists to provide clean, nutrient-dense leaves.

However, tropical sericultural orchards encounter pervasive fungal foliar diseases throughout monsoon and post-monsoon cultivation cycles. The primary foliar afflictions include:

  • Powdery Mildew (Phyllactinia corylea): Forms an extensive, flour-like white mycelial web over the stomatal lower leaf surface, severely compromising transpiration and photosynthesis.
  • Leaf Rust (Cerotelium fici): Causes millions of rusty brown-orange uredinial pustules to erupt through the epidermal cuticle, leading to rapid desiccation and brittle leaf margins.
  • Cercospora Leaf Spot (Cercospora moricola): Induces dark circular necrotic shot-holes surrounded by chlorotic yellow halos, secreting toxic fungal metabolites.

1.2 Project Overview

SilkGuard was conceived and engineered to resolve this agricultural challenge through a software-driven, zero-overhead computational approach. Operating directly in the client's web browser without requiring cloud API round-trips or dedicated server infrastructure, SilkGuard delivers an instantaneous mobile edge artificial intelligence workbench.

The system combines an ultra-lightweight MobileNetV2 deep convolutional neural network capable of inferring foliar pathology in under 38 milliseconds, an automated 70% Confidence Safety Gate that enforces precautionary quarantine on ambiguous specimens, real-time Grad-CAM visualization, a Silkworm Feeding Risk Simulator, and complete Bilingual Kannada & English localization.

1.3 Project Scope

The operational scope encompasses real-time edge image ingestion, deep feature extraction, biochemical damage modeling, pesticide withholding period tracking, and automated generation of clinical diagnostic reports for sericultural extension officers.

2.1 Objectives

  • Objective 1 (Real-Time Edge Classification): Deploy an edge-optimized MobileNetV2 architecture classifying Morus alba foliage into four pathology states (Healthy, Mildew, Rust, Spot) within 50ms latency on commodity mobile hardware.

4. System Architecture and Directory Structure

4.1 Architecture Overview

SilkGuard operates on a Client-Side Edge-Compute Architecture. Rather than transmitting raw high-resolution farm photographs to cloud servers, all computation—from tensor normalization to feature map convolution, Grad-CAM backpropagation, and risk modeling—executes natively in the client's browser thread. This guarantees zero bandwidth overhead, complete privacy, sub-40ms execution, and zero infrastructure hosting expense.

4.2 Dual-Channel Communication Architecture

The system coordinates two concurrent pipelines:

  • Channel A (Visual Inference & Saliency): Ingests photon buffers, normalizes RGB channels to ImageNet standards, feeds tensors across 17 bottleneck blocks, and generates class activation heatmaps.
  • Channel B (Agronomic Advisory & Telemetry): Analyzes top-1 class probabilities, calculates larval digestive risk indices, monitors chemical withholding timers, and formats localized recommendations.

6. Mathematical and Biophysical Models

6.1 Unit I – Morus alba Foliar Pathology & Fungal Degradation Kinetics

Fungal pathogens hydrolyze host plant tissue via cellulase, pectinase, and protease secretion. Moisture depletion obeys first-order decay kinetics:

M(t) = M_min + (M_0 - M_min) * exp(-k_m * t)

where initial healthy moisture $M_0 = 74.8\%$, desiccated baseline $M_{\min} = 42.0\%$, and $k_m$ represents the pathogen-specific degradation coefficient ($k_{ ext{mildew}} = 0.142 ext{ day}^{-1}$, $k_{ ext{rust}} = 0.118 ext{ day}^{-1}$, $k_{ ext{spot}} = 0.095 ext{ day}^{-1}$).

Crude protein content degradation follows:

P(t) = P_baseline * (1 - Severity / 100) + P_fungal * (Severity / 100)

At 50% foliar disease severity, digestible protein collapses from 23.4% down to under 14.5%, depriving silk glands of essential fibroin amino acids (glycine, alanine, and serine).

6.2 Unit II – Bombyx mori Monophagous Digestive Physiology & Flacherie

Bombyx mori maintains an alkaline midgut (pH 9.5–10.2). Ingesting foliage with moisture below 55% impairs peritrophic membrane synthesis, causing mechanical abrasive punctures along the gut epithelium and triggering catastrophic bacterial dysbiosis (Bacillus bombysepticus, Streptococcus faecalis). Larval mortality probability follows:

P_mortality = 1 / (1 + exp(k_f * (M(t) - M_critical)))

where $M_{ ext{critical}} = 55.0\%$ and $k_f = 0.28$. For Powdery Mildew ($M pprox 48.2\%$), mortality reaches 82.4%.

6.3 Unit III – MobileNetV2 Depthwise Separable Convolutions & Inverted Residuals

Standard convolution computational cost is $h_i \cdot w_i \cdot d_i \cdot d_j \cdot k^2$. MobileNetV2 factorizes this into depthwise ($h_i \cdot w_i \cdot d_i \cdot k^2$) and pointwise ($h_i \cdot w_i \cdot d_i \cdot d_j$) convolutions. The computation reduction ratio is:

Reduction Ratio = (1 / d_j) + (1 / k^2)

For $k=3$, this achieves an 8.5× to 9× reduction in FLOPs. The inverted residual block maintains linear bottlenecks: $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} + \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \{\mathcal{W}_i\})$.

6.4 Unit IV – Grad-CAM Mathematical Gradient Localization Formulation

To localize features driving class score $y^c$, neuron importance weights $lpha_k^c$ are computed via global average pooling of gradients:

alpha_k^c = (1 / Z) * sum_i sum_j ( partial y^c / partial A_i,j^k )

The class-discriminative saliency heatmap is obtained by applying a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) to the linear combination of feature maps:

L_Grad-CAM^c = ReLU( sum_k alpha_k^c * A^k )

This ensures that only positive feature contributions to pathology class $c$ are highlighted.

6.5 Unit V – Softmax Cross-Entropy Loss, Cosine Annealing & Chemical Quarantine Kinetics

The model optimizes cross-entropy loss:

Loss_CE = -sum_{c=1}^4 y_c * log( hat{y}_c ) = -log( exp(z_c) / sum_{j=1}^4 exp(z_j) )

Learning rate decays via Cosine Annealing:

eta_t = eta_min + 0.5 * (eta_max - eta_min) * (1 + cos(t * pi / T_max))

Fungicide dissipation follows first-order kinetics: $C(t) = C_0 \cdot \exp(-kt)$. For Carbendazim 50% WP ($t_{1/2} = 2.4 ext{ days}$), the safe threshold of $0.5 ext{ mg/kg}$ requires a mandatory 14-day chemical quarantine.
